# Notifications

**Settings → Notifications** has four toggles.

| Notification | Default | Fires when |
|---|---|---|
| **Discipline Rule Violations** | On | An order is blocked by a rule |
| **Revenge Cooldown Active** | On | The cooldown arms after a stop-out |
| **Daily Loss Limit Approaching** | On | You reach 80% of your maximum daily loss |
| **Trade Confirmation Toast** | **Off** | Every order is placed or filled |

## Why trade confirmations are off by default

Because a toast on every fill trains you to watch fills.

The whole design of this product pushes attention away from moment-to-moment execution and toward process. A notification each time an order goes through pulls it straight back. It is available if you want it, but the default is deliberate.

## The three worth keeping on

**Discipline Rule Violations** — you want to know immediately why an order was refused. This is the notification that teaches.

**Revenge Cooldown Active** — the cooldown arming means you just stopped out. Knowing the clock has started is a useful prompt to step away rather than sit refreshing.

**Daily Loss Limit Approaching** — firing at 80% of your cap, this is the single most valuable alert in the list. It arrives while you can still choose to stop, rather than after the door has closed.

## A separate set of toggles

**Discipline Mode → Settings** has its own notification switches, covering remaining trades, risk usage, cooldown, score drops, blocked trades, streak milestones and tier progress.

These are **stored in your browser** for that device only. They are not synced to your account and are independent of the four above.

If you use StrikeFluency on two machines, these will differ between them. The Settings page notifications are server-side and follow you.

## What is not available

There is no email, SMS or push notification. Everything is in-app, which means alerts only reach you while the app is open.

Practically: the daily loss warning cannot reach you if you have closed the tab. Which, if you have hit 80% of your loss cap, is arguably the ideal outcome anyway.
