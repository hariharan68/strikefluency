# Themes and layout

**Settings → Customization.**

## The four themes

| Theme | Description |
|---|---|
| **Obsidian Dark** | The original dark theme |
| **Misty Teal** | Light, cool-toned |
| **Forest Paper** | Light, warm-toned |
| **Aqua Cloud** | Light, bright |

One dark and three lights. All four are complete themes, not variations — every colour in the app is defined per theme, so nothing is unreadable or half-styled in any of them.

## The quick toggle

The sun/moon button in the top bar flips between **dark** and **whichever light theme you last used**.

So if you chose Forest Paper, the toggle switches between Obsidian Dark and Forest Paper. It does not cycle through all four — pick your light theme once in Settings, then use the toggle for day-to-day switching.

The same toggle appears in the documentation header and on the marketing pages.

## Sidebar layout

Two modes:

**Default** — icons with text labels. Clear, wider.

**Icon Rail** — icons only, at 72px wide, with labels appearing on hover. Reclaims about 170px of horizontal space.

Icon Rail applies on **wide screens only** (roughly 1181px and above). Below that the sidebar adapts on its own regardless of this setting.

If you spend most of your time on the option chain or the strategy builder, Icon Rail is worth it — both of those screens use every pixel of width you can give them.

## Collapsing the sidebar

Independently of layout mode, the sidebar can be fully collapsed with the small circular toggle on its edge. That state is also remembered per device.

## Per-device, not per-account

This is the part that surprises people:

**Theme and layout are stored in your browser, not on your account.**

Your laptop and your desktop can have different themes, and changing one does not change the other. Signing in on a new machine gives you the defaults.

This differs from every other setting — trading preferences and notifications are server-side and follow you everywhere.

The reasoning is that visual preference is genuinely device-dependent. A theme that reads well on a bright office monitor is not the one you want on a laptop at night.

## Responsive behaviour

Below about 1180px the sidebar narrows to an icon rail automatically. Below 820px it becomes a horizontal strip at the top. Below 560px the brand text is hidden.

These happen regardless of your layout setting — they are the app adapting, not a preference.
