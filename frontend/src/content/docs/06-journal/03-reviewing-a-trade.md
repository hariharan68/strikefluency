# Reviewing a trade

Open any journal entry to see the full record and add your notes.

## The two written fields

### Pre-trade thesis

What you were thinking going in. The field prompts you for:

> Setup, market context, entry trigger, invalidation, and intended exit.

Those five things are a complete trade plan. If you cannot fill them in after the fact, you did not have a plan — which is itself the most useful thing the exercise can tell you.

**Invalidation** is the one people skip. It is the answer to "what would tell me I am wrong?", and a trade without one has no logical stop level.

### Post-trade review

What actually happened. The prompt:

> What went well? What went wrong? Why did it happen? What will you do differently?

The third question is the one that matters. "I exited early" is an observation. "I exited early because the position was larger than I was comfortable with" is a cause, and causes are fixable.

## Marking a trade reviewed

There is a **reviewed** flag, and the journal tracks your **review completion rate** as a headline metric.

Entries show one of three states:

- `Needs review` — untouched
- `Review started` — partially filled
- `Reviewed` — done

The **Needs review** quick view filters to everything outstanding. Working that list to empty at the end of each week is the single best habit available in this product.

## What a good review looks like

Short. Three or four sentences.

> Bought 24500 CE on a level reclaim after the 10:15 pullback. Invalidation was a close back under 24480. Exited at target for +₹1,840.
>
> Followed the plan exactly. Slight hesitation on entry cost me about four points — I waited for a second confirmation candle that I had not planned for. Next time, take the level as planned or skip it.

That is enough. Long reviews do not get written; short honest ones do.

## Reviewing losers and winners

Review both. The temptation is to study losses and skim wins, but wins conceal the more dangerous errors — an oversized position that worked, a missing stop that did not matter this time.

A useful discipline: for every winning trade, ask "was this good process or a good outcome?" They are not the same and only one of them repeats.

## Weekly rhythm

The mechanics are automatic, so a working rhythm is:

- **Daily** — tag emotion and mistake on the day's trades. Two minutes.
- **Weekly** — write reviews on anything still marked *Needs review*, and look at the mistake breakdown. Twenty minutes.
- **Monthly** — check the discipline trend and performance by setup. Half an hour.

That is roughly two hours a month, and it is the highest-return time you will spend on trading.
